Jacob March

I work on quantum information and quantum gravity, using holography to understand how the entanglement structure of a quantum field theory is encoded in the geometry of spacetime.

I'm a physics Ph.D. student at Northeastern University, advised by Prof. Ning Bao. Recent work covers reflected entropy and tensor networks in 3D gravity, holographic quantum secret sharing, and entanglement at finite resources. I'm also developing machine-learning methods for quantum error correction and for the spectral statistics of the SYK model. Before Northeastern, I studied mathematics and physics at Grinnell College.

Research

What I work on

Current

Holographic entanglement

Reflected entropy from coarse-grained tensor networks in 3D gravity, phase transitions and uberholography of pure-state geometries, and the capacity of entanglement when resources are finite.

Current

Holographic quantum secret sharing

The combinatorial structure of secret-sharing schemes in holography: which collections of boundary regions can recover information encoded in the bulk.

Current

Machine learning for quantum codes

Graph neural network decoders for the triangular color code, structured by its LightsOut formulation, and learned decoders for non-abelian topological codes.

Current

SYK spectral statistics

Normalizing flows that learn the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev spectral distribution, to extrapolate beyond exact diagonalization to large N and probe deviations from random-matrix theory.

Earlier work

Grinnell College

Quantum fields around black holes

2D holographic descriptions of 4D Kerr and Kerr–Newman–AdS black holes from scalar probes, and finite-element calculations of non-spherically-symmetric bound states, which fed into a paper on superradiant suppression in conformal Weyl gravity.

CMS · École Polytechnique

Jet substructure

Monte Carlo studies at the Laboratoire Leprince-Ringuet showing that secondary Lund jet planes isolate gluon-enriched jet samples, contributing analysis to a JHEP (2024) study.
